Single Mom, Two Small Kids, and a Big Idea

When I landed back in Canada at 4 am from Brazil in 2012 with 2 kids, ages 2 and 3, 13 pieces of luggage, 2 strollers, and 2 car seats, I didn’t think I’d make it.

My very short marriage ended, and I wanted to come home. I became a single mom instantly. My days were full. My nights were fuller. My to-do list was longer than a grocery store receipt, and I was always exhausted.

But somewhere in the middle of all that, I kept thinking: I need to write my book. I spent years training leaders in Latin and Central America, in both Spanish and Portuguese, and I was really good at it. I knew that if I could just capture my method—the way I trained, the way I broke through to people—I would be able to help millions.

When I came back to North America, once again, I started training in the corporate world, this time, not as a consultant but as an employee. I wanted to build my knowledge base here. So while the kids adjusted to their new home and learned to speak English, I went to work. I commuted two hours each way and took care of my kids. I was a rat on a wheel for eight solid years. And then, COVID happened, and we all got locked down.

Six Corporate Clients in the First Month

Exactly on February 7th, 2022, I was let go from my corporate training job. My department was cut, and soon after, the whole company was bought out. I was jobless and a little scared; I’m not gonna lie.

Funny enough, the day I was laid off was the same day I hit publish on Amazon, and my book went officially LIVE. This is the part that still makes me smile. God never closes one door without opening up another. I had a decision to make: Do I look for another job, or do I go back to consulting? I was in the middle of COVID, I was the only breadwinner, and I had two little ones to feed. I decided to take a deep breath, trust God, and let go of the J.O.B mentality.

I promised myself I would never go back to an office again. I’m pleased to tell you that I didn’t. From the day I made the decision to start my consulting business, I work from home, wherever that happens to be.

Research shows that when you take a stand for your life and make what I call a “no going back decision,” that decision affects the molecular structure of the universe around us.

So, here’s what that decision brought about:

In the first month after publishing, I signed six corporate clients to teach leadership. Six. In one month, I landed a 4-year contract with one of the biggest publishing companies that literally took care of all my bills.

Your roadmap · 5 steps

The PROVE
Method

Expertise in, clients out. Come back to this page whenever you need to see where you are.

P
Step 01

Pinpoint the Problem

Who do you help, and what do they need help with? We’re getting specific here. Your book needs to speak to someone who reads it and thinks, She gets me.

R
Step 02

Reveal Your Method

Get what you know out of your head and onto the page. We’ll turn the way you help people into clear steps your readers can follow. You may think, But I just know how to do this. Exactly. Let’s show them how.

O
Step 03

Offer the Evidence

Give people a reason to trust your approach. Share examples, client stories, and results that show what your method can do. Help readers see how it could apply to their own situation.

V
Step 04

Value Your Expertise

Decide what readers can hire you to help them achieve. We’ll build a paid offer with a clear outcome, scope, and price. You need to know what you’re offering when someone finishes your book and asks, How can I work with you?

E
Step 05

Engage Your Future Clients

Get your book into the right hands and keep the conversation going. Use your launch, your teaching, and your follow-up to build relationships with people who could benefit from your help.

Traditional Publishing Is for Famous People

When I published my first book, I needed it to do something. I had two kids, bills to pay, and a corporate job that disappeared the same day my book went live. A beautiful book sitting on a shelf would have been lovely, but lovely wasn’t going to take care of my family. I needed people to read it, understand how I could help them, and pick up the phone.

And they did. Six corporate clients in the first month. My book gave people a chance to get to know my work before we ever had a conversation. That’s why I want you to think about publishing a little differently. You’re an expert. You have something to teach, a problem you solve, and people who need your help. Your book should make it easier for those people to find you.

But first, we need to talk about the dream. You know the one where a publisher discovers you, falls in love with your ideas, and offers you a contract. They handle everything, your book lands in bookstores, and suddenly everyone wants to interview you.
